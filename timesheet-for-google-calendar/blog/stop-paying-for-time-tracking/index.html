Stop Paying $144/Year for Time Tracking

If you’re a freelancer using Harvest, Toggl, or any other paid time tracker, ask yourself: do I actually need a separate app to track time I’m already tracking in Google Calendar?

The expensive time tracking trap

Here’s a scenario that plays out every day. A freelance consultant blocks out “Client A — Strategy Meeting” in Google Calendar from 10:00 to 11:30 AM. Then they open Harvest or Toggl and type “Strategy Meeting, Client A, 1.5 hours.”

They entered the same information twice. Once in the tool they actually use to manage their day (Calendar), and once in the tool that generates invoices (time tracker). That duplication costs time and introduces errors — and they’re paying over $100/year for the privilege.

What you’re actually paying for

Time tracking apps like Harvest and Toggl are powerful tools built for teams of 10-100+ people who need approval workflows, team dashboards, project budgeting, and Jira integrations. They’re enterprise software priced for enterprises.

But if you’re a solo freelancer, a small agency of 2-3 people, or a consultant who just needs to turn calendar hours into invoices, you’re paying for features you’ll never use. What you actually need is simpler: take the events in my calendar, calculate the hours, apply my rates, and give me a spreadsheet.

The Google Calendar approach

The idea is simple: use the tool you already use. Most freelancers already block time in Google Calendar for client work. Many already color-code by client. The data is there — it just needs to be extracted and formatted.

Google Calendar events have everything a timesheet needs: date, start time, end time (duration is calculated), event title (description of work), and event color (project category). The only thing missing is the hourly rate, which stays constant per client anyway.

What this looks like in practice

Here’s a typical week for a freelance consultant:

  • Monday: 3 events colored red (Client A), 1 event colored green (Client B)
  • Tuesday: 2 red events, 1 blue event (internal/non-billable)
  • Wednesday: 1 green event, 2 red events
  • Thursday: 4 red events
  • Friday: 1 green event, 1 uncolored event (“Acme meeting”)

With a tool like Timesheet for Google Calendar, those events become a formatted spreadsheet in one click:

  • Red → Client A at $150/hr
  • Green → Client B at $125/hr
  • Blue → Internal at $0/hr
  • “Acme” (keyword match) → Client A at $150/hr

Total hours, total billing per project, and a summary tab ready for invoicing. No manual entry, no second app.

When should you use Harvest or Toggl instead? If you manage a team of 5+ people, need project budgeting, approval workflows, or integrations with project management tools like Jira, then the enterprise tools are worth the price. But if you’re a solo freelancer, a 2-3 person agency, or a consultant who just needs calendar-to-timesheet, you’re dramatically overpaying.

The workflow: 60 seconds, every billing cycle

  1. During the week: Use Google Calendar as you normally do. Color events by client.
  2. End of billing period: Open your timesheet spreadsheet
  3. One click: Extensions → Timesheet for Google Calendar → Refresh Timesheet
  4. Review: Check the Summary tab for totals, Review tab for any flagged issues
  5. Invoice: Copy the numbers into your invoice template (or share the sheet directly)

Total time: under 60 seconds. No app switching, no manual entry, no data duplication.
