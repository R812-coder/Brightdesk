How to Track Billable Hours in Google Calendar

You’re already blocking time for client work in Google Calendar. With the right system, those blocks become your billable hours — no separate time tracker needed.

Why your calendar is already a time tracker

Think about what a time tracker actually records: the date, the start time, the end time, and a description of the work. Now look at your Google Calendar. Every event already has all four of those data points. The only thing missing is the hourly rate, which stays constant per client anyway.

The problem isn’t that you aren’t tracking your time. It’s that Google Calendar doesn’t have a “generate timesheet” button. So you end up re-entering the same data into Harvest, Toggl, or a manual spreadsheet — duplicating work you’ve already done.

The solution is to treat your calendar as your single source of truth for billable hours, then extract the data when it’s time to invoice.

Set up a color-coding system

Google Calendar has 11 event colors. The most effective billing system assigns one color per client or project. This takes one extra click when you create an event, and it pays off every billing cycle.

Here’s a system that works well for most freelancers:

  • Tomato (red) — Your highest-value client
  • Basil (green) — Second client
  • Peacock (blue) — Third client
  • Banana (yellow) — Fourth client
  • Graphite (gray) — Internal / non-billable work

Right-click any event in Google Calendar and select a color from the palette. Once you build this habit, you’ll have a visually scannable week where you can immediately see how your time is distributed across clients.

Consistency matters more than the specific colors. Pick a system and stick with it. Don’t change which color means which client mid-project — it makes extraction harder later.

Event naming conventions that help billing

A good event title does two things: it reminds you what you worked on, and it gives your client a clear line item on their invoice. Here are naming patterns that work:

  • Client Name — Task: “Acme Corp — Homepage Redesign”
  • Client Name — Category: “Acme Corp — Design Review”
  • Project Code — Description: “ACM-042 — Wireframe Revisions”

Avoid vague titles like “Work” or “Meeting.” When you generate a timesheet two weeks later, you won’t remember what “Meeting” was about, and your client won’t accept it on an invoice either.

Extract billable hours into a spreadsheet

At the end of your billing period, you need to pull the data out of Google Calendar and into a format you can invoice from. There are two approaches.

The manual method

Open Google Calendar in one tab and a Google Sheet in another. Go through each day, and for each billable event, type the date, start time, end time, client name, and task description into the spreadsheet. Add a formula to calculate duration: =(end_time - start_time) * 24. Multiply by your hourly rate.

This works, but it takes 20-30 minutes per week and is error-prone. You’ll inevitably miss events, mistype times, or forget to account for recurring meetings.

The automated method

5 habits for accurate billable hour tracking

1. Color events as you create them

Don’t batch-color at the end of the week. One extra click when creating the event saves 10 minutes of guesswork later.

2. Create events for unscheduled work

If a client calls you for 20 minutes, add a calendar event after the call. Otherwise that time vanishes from your records. Many freelancers undercharge by 10-15% simply because they don’t log ad-hoc work.

3. Use descriptive titles

“Client A — API Integration” is billable. “Stuff” is not. Your event titles become your invoice line items.

4. Review before invoicing

Before sending an invoice, scan for uncategorized events (events without a color or keyword match), overlapping events (potential double-billing), and suspiciously short events (accidental calendar entries). A good timesheet tool flags these for you.

5. Bill at the same time every period

Pick a cadence — weekly or monthly — and generate your timesheet at the same time every cycle. Last Friday of the month, open your sheet, one-click refresh, review, invoice. The routine is what makes it reliable.
